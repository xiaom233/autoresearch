### Weakness 2

**Response.** We fine-tuned a 31M-parameter, project-pretrained DFPIR-blinded model on each scenario using AutoIR's final estimated degradation and the same adaptation-training GPU-time budget (4.248 GPU-hours per scenario on average). Both methods select checkpoints using support PSNR.

|---|---:|---:|
| AutoIR | 24.968 | 22.956 |
| DFPIR-blinded before adaptation | 23.995 | 22.030 |
| DFPIR-blinded-ft, support-selected best | 25.650 | 23.616 |

### Weakness 4

> The Auto Research term is a big word and seems to be over-claimed. The demonstrated system operates within predefined degradation and architecture toolkits. This is indeed useful automation to some extent, but the boundary between agent decisions and manually encoded rules is unclear

**Response.** We use Auto Research to describe the automation of a **hypothesis–experiment–evaluation–revision loop**, consistent with the research workflows explored by The AI Scientist and Coscientist (Lu et al., 2024; Boiko et al., 2023). AutoIR instantiates this pattern for scenario-specific model development in low-level vision: DC formulates and verifies executable degradation hypotheses; RS constructs synthetic training data and configures and trains a restorer; CLR evaluates restoration and training feedback to revise the hypothesis or model. We therefore position AutoIR as a domain-specific instantiation of Auto Research focused on restoration-model development.

This differs from restoration agents such as AgenticIR, which select and sequence pretrained restoration models to process images. AutoIR develops a scenario-specific restorer through DC–RS–CLR, and its output includes a trained model that can subsequently be deployed. The human-provided toolkits define the available operators, model components and resource constraints; the agent makes the scenario-specific hypotheses, configurations and experimental revisions within that space. We will clarify this scope and distinguish automated restoration-model development from pretrained-model orchestration and broader autonomous scientific discovery.

The restorer is trained exclusively on DIV2K training-set clean images, with LQ inputs synthesized online using the estimated degradation pipeline. No SIDD images are used for gradient-based training. Testing uses 1,120 real SIDD patches from 35 scenes disjoint from the support scenes.

|---|---:|---:|---:|

| DFPIR, official release 5D checkpoint† | 23.291 | 0.3409 | 0.5888 |

## Reviewer Tqi7

### Weakness 1

> Limited methodological novelty and design autonomy. Agentic planning/reflection [1] and restoration-driven degradation correction [2] have precedents. AutoIR integrates these ideas with restorer training, but RS mainly selects predefined modules around a Swin backbone. Its reported DIV2K gain without CLR is 0.34 dB (Table 2). The demonstrated contribution is useful workflow automation, while the claimed advance in autonomous model design remains modest. The distinctive technical mechanism beyond this integration needs clearer articulation.

**Response.** AutoIR's core contribution is a framework for automating the complete image-restoration model-development cycle. The contribution of AutoIR is more than a restorer training protocol. To our knowledge, it is the first LLM-driven IR framework to close this full loop: **executable degradation modeling (DC) → synthetic training-data generation → restorer configuration and training (RS) → feedback-driven revision of both the degradation pipeline and restorer (CLR)**. This opens a research direction in which the system develops a scenario-specific restoration model from paired observations.

We acknowledge that the current RS stage configures predefined modules rather than independently inventing new architectures, and its average architectural gain is modest. However, the reported 0.34 dB is averaged over 24 degradation scenarios and can mask larger benefits in individual scenarios. In Supplementary Section 1 (Case Study), keeping the estimated degradation chain fixed as contrast stretch (4) + JPEG (5), changing the restorer from Swin to Swin+SG+GCM improves DIV2K PSNR from 17.46 to 19.56 dB (**+2.10 dB**) under the same training budget. This illustrates that the benefit of architecture specialization can be substantial for particular degradations, even when its overall average is smaller. More open-ended LLM-driven architecture design is itself an important subproblem within the proposed research loop and remains a direction for further investigation.

The DC and CLR cases in Supplementary Section 1 further illustrate the agent's evidence-guided reasoning. In the DC case, removing a false-positive blur operator while retaining Swin+SG improves PSNR from 22.65 to 26.64 dB (+3.99 dB). In the CLR case, poor restoration prompts the agent to revisit the training degradation and replace Gaussian blur with glass blur, again retaining Swin+SG. PSNR rises from 21.75 to 27.20 dB (+5.45 dB over the preceding round and +1.70 dB over the previous best). A subsequent hypothesis adding Gaussian blur gives 27.03 dB, illustrating that further revision need not help. Together with the RS case, these trajectories demonstrate how the agent uses evidence and experimental feedback to revise the degradation model, training distribution and network configuration within the complete development loop.

### Weakness 2

> Degradation estimation remains a bottleneck for self-correction. An incorrect hypothesis produces mismatched synthetic training data. In the impulse-noise failure case, underestimated noise severity and a spurious brightness transformation persist through CLR, and architecture changes do not improve restoration (Supplement, Section 2; Table 2). This illustrates how refinement can stall when diagnostic signals are unreliable. A stronger mechanism for identifying the source of restoration errors would improve CLR.

**Response.** Estimating a composite degradation chain from a few paired observations is an ill-posed inverse problem. Operators interact: one degradation can obscure or imitate the diagnostic signature of another, and different chains can produce similar observations. Recovering the exact types, severities and order is therefore difficult and merits dedicated research.

The impulse-noise case exposes this ambiguity. Strong corruption distorts the photometric statistics used to diagnose brightness, while independent noise realizations weaken pixelwise simulation verification. CLR retains an underestimated noise severity and a spurious brightness operation; changing the architecture cannot compensate for the resulting training-distribution mismatch. Training stability, checkpoint behavior and support-pair verification guide revision, but do not guarantee correct error attribution.

AutoIR's contribution is to provide the complete agent-driven restoration-development loop in which degradation hypotheses, synthetic training data and the restorer can be tested and revised together. The reported failure identifies a subproblem within that loop: more reliable degradation identification and uncertainty-aware attribution of restoration errors. These can be studied independently and incorporated into DC/CLR to strengthen the full workflow. We will make both this research opportunity and the current limitation explicit.

### Specific Points of Feedback for Rebuttal

#### Question 1

> What distinctive reasoning or model-design mechanism does AutoIR contribute beyond its predefined toolkit? Please illustrate with an existing example.

**Response.** The RS case in the submitted supplement (blind_0018) directly illustrates evidence-guided restorer development. After verification supported the contrast-stretch (severity 4) + JPEG (severity 5) hypothesis, RS retained this estimated degradation chain and changed the network from plain Swin to Swin+SimpleGate+FiLM-GCM, activating global modulation for the contrast-related degradation. Both configurations used the same two-epoch training budget.

| Restorer configuration | DIV2K PSNR | LSDIR PSNR |
|---|---:|---:|
| Swin | 17.46 | 17.31 |
| Swin+SimpleGate+FiLM-GCM | 19.56 | 18.89 |
| Improvement | **+2.10** | **+1.58** |

Here the performance improvement accompanies a network-configuration change under the same estimated training degradation. The mechanism is to use diagnostic evidence and confidence to guide restorer configuration, train the resulting network and evaluate its behavior within the DC–RS–CLR development loop. The building blocks are predefined; the automated development decisions determine when and how to use them.

#### Question 2

> How does CLR distinguish an incorrect degradation hypothesis from an inadequate restorer, and why does it fail to correct the reported impulse-noise case?

**Response.** Composite degradation identification is ill-posed because coupled operators can imitate or obscure each other's signatures. CLR combines two kinds of evidence: support-pair simulation discrepancies inform DC revision, while training curves, divergence and checkpoint behavior inform model/training revision. Neither is a causal oracle. In the impulse case, strong corruption distorts brightness-related statistics and independent noise realizations undermine pixelwise verification. The underestimated noise and spurious brightness hypothesis persist, producing mismatched synthetic training data that architecture changes cannot repair. Reliable attribution is therefore a distinct research problem that can strengthen the DC–RS–CLR loop; the present failure remains unresolved.
